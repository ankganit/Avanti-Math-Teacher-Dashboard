Differential Equation: Basics
Definition

An equation that contains the independent variable, the dependent variable and derivatives of the dependent variable.

$$\frac{dy}{dx}+y=\sin x$$
Types
  • Ordinary (ODE): only one independent variable.
  • Partial (PDE): more than one independent variable.

JEE deals with ODEs only.

Order
Order of the highest-order derivative present in the equation.
Degree
Power of the highest-order derivative after the equation is made a polynomial in its derivatives (no radicals or fractions of derivatives).
Degree not defined when the derivatives sit inside functions that cannot be removed, e.g. $\sin y'$, $\cos y''$.

Formation of a Differential Equation
n independent arbitrary constants  ⟹  DE of order n
Method
1
Differentiate the family n times.
2
Eliminate the n constants from the n+1 equations.
FamilyDifferential equation
$y=mx$$y=x\dfrac{dy}{dx}$
$x^2+y^2=a^2$$x+y\dfrac{dy}{dx}=0$
$y^2=4ax$$y=2x\dfrac{dy}{dx}$
$y=Ae^{2x}+Be^{-3x}$$y''+y'-6y=0$
Watch out: constants must be independent. In $y=ae^{x+b}$ we have $y=ce^{x}$ (with $c=ae^{b}$), so there is only one constant and the order is 1.
3
Solution of a Differential Equation
General solution
Contains n independent arbitrary constants (n = order). It represents a family of curves.
Particular solution
Constants given definite values using conditions. It represents one curve from the family.
Singular solution
A solution that cannot be obtained from the general solution by any choice of constants.
Example: $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2\sqrt{y}$  ⟹  general: $y=(x+C)^2$.
$y=0$ also satisfies it, but no $C$ gives it: singular.
Initial value problem

$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=3x^2,\ y(1)=2$  ⟹  $y=x^3+C$, and $2=1+C$ gives $C=1$, so $y=x^3+1$.

Tip: use the given condition at the end, after integrating, to find $C$.
3
Variable Separation Method

When the equation can be written as

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=f(x)\,g(y)$$

separate the variables, then integrate:

$$\int\frac{dy}{g(y)}=\int f(x)\,dx+C$$
Examples
1. $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=e^{x-y}$  ⟹  $e^{y}dy=e^{x}dx$  ⟹  $\boldsymbol{e^{y}=e^{x}+C}$
2. $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{1+y^2}{1+x^2}$  ⟹  $\boldsymbol{\tan^{-1}y-\tan^{-1}x=C}$
3. $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=xy,\ y(0)=1$  ⟹  $\ln y=\dfrac{x^2}{2}+C$, and $C=0$, so $\boldsymbol{y=e^{x^2/2}}$
Tips:
  • Dividing by $g(y)$ can lose constant solutions where $g(y)=0$. Check them.
  • Write the constant as $\ln C$ when logs appear.

Homogeneous Differential Equation
$$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{f(x,y)}{g(x,y)}=F\!\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)$$

$f,g$ homogeneous of the same degree: $f(\lambda x,\lambda y)=\lambda^{n}f(x,y)$.

Method
1
Put $y=vx$, so $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=v+x\dfrac{dv}{dx}$.
2
Separate the variables:
$$\frac{dv}{F(v)-v}=\frac{dx}{x}$$
3
Integrate, then put back $v=\dfrac{y}{x}$.

If the form is $\dfrac{dx}{dy}=F\!\left(\dfrac{x}{y}\right)$, put $x=vy$ instead.

Ex. $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{x^2+y^2}{2xy}$. With $y=vx$: $x\dfrac{dv}{dx}=\dfrac{1-v^2}{2v}$, so $\dfrac{2v\,dv}{1-v^2}=\dfrac{dx}{x}$,
giving $\boldsymbol{x^2-y^2=Cx}$
4
Reducible to Homogeneous
$$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{a_1x+b_1y+c_1}{a_2x+b_2y+c_2}$$
Case 1:  $\dfrac{a_1}{a_2}\neq\dfrac{b_1}{b_2}$
Lines intersect at $(h,k)$. Put $x=X+h,\ y=Y+k$. The equation becomes homogeneous in $X,Y$.

Ex. $\dfrac{x-y+1}{x+y-3}$: the lines meet at $(1,2)$, so use $x=X+1,\ y=Y+2$ to get $\dfrac{dY}{dX}=\dfrac{X-Y}{X+Y}$.

Case 2:  $\dfrac{a_1}{a_2}=\dfrac{b_1}{b_2}$
Lines are parallel. Put $z=a_1x+b_1y$. The equation becomes variable separable in $x,z$.
Ex. $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{x+y+1}{x+y-1}$. Put $z=x+y$: $\dfrac{dz}{dx}=\dfrac{2z}{z-1}$,
so $\dfrac{z-1}{2z}dz=dx$, giving $\boldsymbol{y-x-\ln|x+y|=C}$
5
Linear Differential Equation
$$\frac{dy}{dx}+P\,y=Q\qquad(P,Q\text{ are functions of }x)$$
$$\text{I.F.}=e^{\int P\,dx}$$ $$y\cdot(\text{I.F.})=\int Q\cdot(\text{I.F.})\,dx+C$$
Linear in $x$
$$\frac{dx}{dy}+P\,x=Q\quad(P,Q\text{ functions of }y)$$ $$\text{I.F.}=e^{\int P\,dy},\qquad x\cdot(\text{I.F.})=\int Q\cdot(\text{I.F.})\,dy+C$$
Ex.1 $\dfrac{dy}{dx}+\dfrac{y}{x}=x^2$. I.F. $=x$, so $xy=\dfrac{x^4}{4}+C$, i.e. $\boldsymbol{y=\dfrac{x^3}{4}+\dfrac{C}{x}}$
Ex.2 $(x+y)\dfrac{dy}{dx}=1$ gives $\dfrac{dx}{dy}-x=y$. I.F. $=e^{-y}$, so $\boldsymbol{x=Ce^{y}-y-1}$
Tip: do not add a constant while finding the I.F.

Reducible to Linear + I.F. Table
Bernoulli's equation
$$\frac{dy}{dx}+Py=Q\,y^{n}\qquad(n\neq0,1)$$

Divide by $y^{n}$ and put $z=y^{1-n}$. It becomes linear:

$$\frac{dz}{dx}+(1-n)P\,z=(1-n)Q$$

Ex. $\dfrac{dy}{dx}+\dfrac{y}{x}=y^2$. Put $z=\dfrac1y$: $\dfrac{dz}{dx}-\dfrac{z}{x}=-1$, giving $\boldsymbol{\dfrac{1}{xy}=C-\ln|x|}$

Also: if $f'(y)\dfrac{dy}{dx}+P f(y)=Q$, put $f(y)=z$.

General Form of Variable Separation
A. Substitution
$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=f(ax+by+c)$: put $z=ax+by+c$, then $z'=a+b\,f(z)$.
$y\,f(xy)\,dx+x\,g(xy)\,dy=0$: put $xy=v$.
B. Exact differentials to spot
$x\,dy+y\,dx$$=d(xy)$
$\dfrac{x\,dy-y\,dx}{x^2}$$=d\!\left(\dfrac yx\right)$
$\dfrac{y\,dx-x\,dy}{y^2}$$=d\!\left(\dfrac xy\right)$
$\dfrac{x\,dy-y\,dx}{xy}$$=d\!\left(\ln\dfrac yx\right)$
$\dfrac{x\,dy-y\,dx}{x^2+y^2}$$=d\!\left(\tan^{-1}\dfrac yx\right)$
$x\,dx+y\,dy$$=\tfrac12\,d(x^2+y^2)$
Ex.1 $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=(x+y)^2$: put $z=x+y$, so $\dfrac{dz}{dx}=1+z^2$ and $\boldsymbol{x+y=\tan(x+C)}$
Ex.2 $x\,dy-y\,dx=(x^2+y^2)dx$ gives $d\!\left(\tan^{-1}\dfrac yx\right)=dx$, so $\boldsymbol{\tan^{-1}\dfrac yx=x+C}$

Applications: Geometry

Let $y'=\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ at the point $P(x,y)$.

Slope of tangent $=y'$  |  Slope of normal $=-\dfrac{1}{y'}$
Tangent: $Y-y=y'(X-x)$
Length of tangent$\left|\dfrac{y\sqrt{1+y'^2}}{y'}\right|$
Length of normal$\left|y\sqrt{1+y'^2}\right|$
Subtangent$\left|\dfrac{y}{y'}\right|$
Subnormal$\left|y\,y'\right|$
Orthogonal trajectories
1
Form the DE of the given family (eliminate $c$).
2
Replace $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ by $-\dfrac{dx}{dy}$.
3
Solve the new DE.

Ex. $y=cx$ gives $y'=\dfrac yx$. Replacing: $-\dfrac{dx}{dy}=\dfrac yx$, so $x\,dx+y\,dy=0$, giving $\boldsymbol{x^2+y^2=C}$

Applications: Rate Problems
Growth & decay
$$\frac{dN}{dt}=kN\ \Rightarrow\ N=N_0e^{kt}$$

$k>0$ growth, $k<0$ decay. Half-life $T_{1/2}=\dfrac{\ln 2}{|k|}$.

Newton's law of cooling
$$\frac{dT}{dt}=-k\,(T-T_s)\ \Rightarrow\ T=T_s+(T_0-T_s)e^{-kt}$$

$T_s$ = surroundings, $T_0$ = initial temperature.

Mixing (tank) problems
$\dfrac{dQ}{dt}=$ (rate in) $-$ (rate out), where rate out $=\dfrac{Q}{V}\times$ outflow rate.
Ex.1 Bacteria double in 3 h. Then $2=e^{3k}$, so 8-fold needs $e^{9k}$: 9 h.
Ex.2 A body at $100^\circ$ in a $20^\circ$ room cools to $60^\circ$ in 10 min. Then $40=80e^{-10k}$, so $e^{-10k}=\tfrac12$. At 20 min: $T=20+80\cdot\tfrac14=\boldsymbol{40^\circ}$
Steps: write the statement as a DE in $t$, solve, then use both conditions to find the constant and $k$.

Tips & Common Mistakes
Avoid these
  • Forgetting $+C$ after integrating.
  • Dropping the modulus in $\ln|x|$.
  • Losing constant solutions while dividing.
  • Adding a constant inside the I.F.
  • Wrong sign of $P$ after writing the standard form.
  • Not clearing radicals before finding degree.
Standard integrals used here
$\displaystyle\int\frac{dx}{x^2+a^2}=\frac1a\tan^{-1}\frac xa$
$\displaystyle\int\frac{dx}{x^2-a^2}=\frac{1}{2a}\ln\left|\frac{x-a}{x+a}\right|$
$\displaystyle\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{a^2-x^2}}=\sin^{-1}\frac xa$
$e^{\ln f(x)}=f(x)$
Before submitting
  • Match the type first, then pick the method.
  • Apply initial conditions only at the end.
  • Differentiate your answer to verify it.
