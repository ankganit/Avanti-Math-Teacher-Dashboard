#General Definition of a Conic
Locus of point $P(x,y)$ whose distance from a fixed point $S$ (Focus) bears a constant ratio $e$ (Eccentricity) to its perpendicular distance from a fixed line $L$ (Directrix).
$\dfrac{SP}{PM} = e \iff SP^2 = e^2 PM^2$
Yaad rakho: Agar Focus directrix par lie kare, toh conic Degenerate ho jata hai (Pair of straight lines)!
#General 2nd Degree Equation & Nature of Conic
General curve: $ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$. Discriminant $\Delta$:
$\Delta = abc + 2fgh - af^2 - bg^2 - ch^2 = \begin{vmatrix} a & h & g \\ h & b & f \\ g & f & c \end{vmatrix}$
ConditionNature of CurveEccentricity ($e$)
$\Delta = 0, h^2 - ab > 0$Pair of intersecting lines—
$\Delta = 0, h^2 - ab = 0$Pair of parallel lines—
$\Delta \neq 0, a = b, h = 0$Circle$e = 0$
$\Delta \neq 0, h^2 = ab$Parabola$e = 1$
$\Delta \neq 0, h^2 < ab$Ellipse$0 < e < 1$
$\Delta \neq 0, h^2 > ab$Hyperbola$e > 1$
$\Delta \neq 0, h^2 > ab, a+b=0$Rectangular Hyperbola$e = \sqrt{2}$
Ex.1 : Classify $x^2 + 4xy + 4y^2 - 6x - 12y + 5 = 0$.
Here $a=1, h=2, b=4 \implies h^2 - ab = 4 - 4 = 0$. Now check $\Delta$:
$\Delta = (1)(4)(5) + 2(-6)(-3)(2) - 1(36) - 4(9) - 5(4) = 20 + 72 - 36 - 36 - 20 = 0$.
Since $\Delta = 0$ and $h^2 = ab$, it represents Parallel lines $(x + 2y - 1)(x + 2y - 5) = 0$.

Equation: $y^2 = 4ax$ ($a > 0$). Standard parameters and comparison:

Focus $(S)$$(a, 0)$$(0, a)$

Dhyan rakho: Agar parabola $(y-k)^2 = 4a(x-h)$ ho toh vertex $(h, k)$ shift ho jata hai!
#Parametric Coordinates of Parabola
Any point on $y^2 = 4ax$ can be represented in terms of single parameter $t$:
$P(t) \equiv (at^2, 2at), \quad t \in \mathbb{R}$
Here $t = \dfrac{1}{m}$, where $m$ is the slope of tangent at $P$! Slope of tangent is $\dfrac{1}{t}$.
Ex.2 : Find vertex, focus, and directrix of $y^2 - 4y - 8x + 12 = 0$.
Completing square: $(y - 2)^2 = 8x - 8 = 8(x - 1) = 4(2)(x - 1) \implies a = 2$.
Vertex: $(h, k) =$ $(1, 2)$. Focus: $(h+a, k) = (1+2, 2) =$ $(3, 2)$.
Directrix: $x - h = -a \implies x - 1 = -2 \implies$ $x + 1 = 0$.
Tip : Length of focal distance of $(at^2, 2at)$ is $SP = a(1 + t^2)$.
Distance between focus $(a, 0)$ and point $(at^2, 2at)$ is $\sqrt{(at^2 - a)^2 + (2at)^2} = a(t^2 + 1)$.

#Properties of Focal Chord ($y^2 = 4ax$)
Chord passing through Focus $S(a, 0)$ with extremities $P(t_1)$ and $Q(t_2)$:
Endpoints condition: $t_1 t_2 = -1$ $\implies$ If one end is $t$, other end is $-1/t$.
Length of Focal Chord: $L = a\left(t + \dfrac{1}{t}\right)^2 = 4a\csc^2\theta$
where $\theta$ is inclination of chord with axis. Minimum length is $4a$ (Latus Rectum at $\theta = 90^\circ$).
Semi-latus rectum is Harmonic Mean of focal segments:
$\dfrac{1}{SP} + \dfrac{1}{SQ} = \dfrac{1}{a} = \dfrac{2}{2a}$ (Super repeated in JEE Main!)
Circle on any focal chord as diameter touches the directrix. Circle on focal distance $SP$ as diameter touches the tangent at vertex.
#Position of Point with Respect to Parabola
Let $S(x, y) = y^2 - 4ax$. For point $P(x_1, y_1)$, calculate $S_1 = y_1^2 - 4ax_1$:
$S_1 > 0$ $\implies$ Point lies Outside the parabola (2 real tangents possible).
$S_1 = 0$ $\implies$ Point lies On the parabola (1 tangent).
$S_1 < 0$ $\implies$ Point lies Inside the parabola (No real tangent).
Ex.3 : If one end of a focal chord of $y^2 = 8x$ is $(2, 4)$, find length of the chord.
Here $4a = 8 \implies a = 2$. Point $(2, 4) \implies 2at = 4 \implies 2(2)t = 4 \implies t = 1$.
Other end has parameter $t_2 = -1/t = -1$.
Length $= a(t + 1/t)^2 = 2(1 + 1)^2 = 2(4) =$ $8$.
Ex.3b : If $SP = 6$ for $y^2 = 12x$, find $SQ$ for focal chord $PQ$.
Here $4a = 12 \implies a = 3$. Harmonic mean relation: $\dfrac{1}{SP} + \dfrac{1}{SQ} = \dfrac{1}{a} = \dfrac{1}{3}$.
$\dfrac{1}{SQ} = \dfrac{1}{3} - \dfrac{1}{6} = \dfrac{1}{6} \implies SQ =$ $6$. (Here $P, Q$ are ends of $LR$!).

#Equations of Tangents to Parabola ($y^2 = 4ax$)
1. Slope Form: Line $y = mx + c$ is tangent to $y^2 = 4ax$ iff $c = \dfrac{a}{m}$
$y = mx + \dfrac{a}{m} \quad (m \neq 0)$ with point of contact $\left(\dfrac{a}{m^2}, \dfrac{2a}{m}\right)$
2. Parametric Form ($t$): $ty = x + at^2$ (Slope is $1/t$)
3. Point Form ($x_1, y_1$): $T = 0 \implies$ $y y_1 = 2a(x + x_1)$
#Point of Intersection of Tangents at $P(t_1)$ and $Q(t_2)$
Solving $t_1 y = x + at_1^2$ and $t_2 y = x + at_2^2$:
$T(h, k) = \Big(a t_1 t_2, \; a(t_1 + t_2)\Big)$
$x$-coord is G.M. of abscissae; $y$-coord is A.M. of ordinates of $P$ and $Q$!
#Director Circle of Parabola
Locus of point of intersection of two mutually perpendicular tangents ($m_1 m_2 = -1$).
From $T(at_1 t_2, a(t_1+t_2))$, perpendicular tangents $\implies t_1 t_2 = -1 \implies x = a(-1) = -a$.
Director circle of a parabola is its Directrix: $x + a = 0$!
Yaad rakho: Parabola ka director circle circle nahi hota, ek straight line (directrix) hoti hai!
Ex.4 : Find common tangent to $y^2 = 4x$ and $x^2 = -32y$.
Tangent to $y^2 = 4x$ ($a=1$): $y = mx + 1/m$. Substitute into $x^2 = -32y$:
$x^2 + 32mx + 32/m = 0$. For tangency: $D = 0 \implies (32m)^2 - 4(32/m) = 0 \implies m^3 = 1/8 \implies m = 1/2$.
Tangent: $y = \dfrac{1}{2}x + 2 \implies$ $x - 2y + 4 = 0$.

#Equations of Normals to Parabola ($y^2 = 4ax$)
1. Parametric Form ($t$): $y + tx = 2at + at^3$ (Slope is $m = -t$)
2. Slope Form ($m$): Put $t = -m$:
$y = mx - 2am - am^3$ with foot of normal $(am^2, -2am)$
3. Point Form ($x_1, y_1$): $y - y_1 = -\dfrac{y_1}{2a}(x - x_1)$
#Co-Normal Points (Three Normals from $(h, k)$)
If normal passes through $(h, k)$: $k = mh - 2am - am^3 \implies$ $am^3 + (2a - h)m + k = 0$.
Roots $m_1, m_2, m_3$ satisfy:
$\sum m_i = m_1 + m_2 + m_3 = 0$ (No $m^2$ term in cubic!)
$\sum m_1 m_2 = \dfrac{2a - h}{a}, \quad m_1 m_2 m_3 = -\dfrac{k}{a}$
Sum of ordinates of co-normal points: $\sum y_i = -2a\sum m_i = 0$
Centroid of $\triangle$ formed by co-normal points: $G = \left(\dfrac{2h - 4a}{3}, \; 0\right)$ (Always on axis!)
Condition for three distinct real normals: $h > 2a$ and $27ak^2 < 4(h - 2a)^3$.
#Relation Between Parameters if Normal at $t_1$ meets at $t_2$
If the normal at $P(t_1)$ intersects the parabola again at $Q(t_2)$:
$t_2 = -t_1 - \dfrac{2}{t_1}$
Minimum length of such a normal chord is $6\sqrt{3}a$ (at $t_1 = \sqrt{2}$).

#Chords of Parabola ($y^2 = 4ax$)
1. Chord joining $P(t_1)$ and $Q(t_2)$:
Slope $= \dfrac{2}{t_1 + t_2}$. Equation: $2x - (t_1 + t_2)y + 2at_1 t_2 = 0$
2. Chord of Contact ($T = 0$): From external point $(x_1, y_1)$
Equation: $y y_1 = 2a(x + x_1)$. Area of $\triangle$ formed by tangents & chord: $\dfrac{(y_1^2 - 4ax_1)^{3/2}}{2a}$.
3. Chord with Given Midpoint $(x_1, y_1)$: $T = S_1$
$y y_1 - 2a(x + x_1) = y_1^2 - 4ax_1 \implies$ $y y_1 - 2ax = y_1^2 - 2ax_1$
#Important Geometric & Reflection Properties
Reflection Property: Any ray parallel to the axis of parabola, after reflection from the inside surface, passes through the Focus $S$.
Tangent at any point $P$ bisects the angle between focal radius $SP$ and perpendicular from $P$ to directrix.
Portion of any tangent intercepted between point of contact and directrix subtends a right angle ($90^\circ$) at the focus.
The locus of foot of perpendicular from focus upon any tangent is the Tangent at vertex ($x = 0$).
Ex.5 : Find midpoint chord of $y^2 = 8x$ whose midpoint is $(3, 2)$.
Here $a = 2$. Using $T = S_1$: $y(2) - 2(2)(x + 3) = (2)^2 - 8(3)$.
$2y - 4x - 12 = 4 - 24 = -20 \implies 2y - 4x + 8 = 0 \implies$ $2x - y - 4 = 0$.
Tip : Subtangent and Subnormal for $y^2 = 4ax$:
Length of subtangent $= 2x_1$ (bisected at vertex). Length of subnormal $= 2a$ (constant for any point!).

#Ellipse: Standard Equations & Terminology
Equation: $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ ($0 < e < 1$). Two standard orientations:
FeatureHorizontal ($a > b$)Vertical ($b > a$)
Eccentricity ($e$)$b^2 = a^2(1 - e^2) \implies e = \sqrt{1 - b^2/a^2}$$a^2 = b^2(1 - e^2) \implies e = \sqrt{1 - a^2/b^2}$
Foci ($S, S'$)$(\pm ae, 0)$$(0, \pm be)$
Directrices$x = \pm \dfrac{a}{e}$$y = \pm \dfrac{b}{e}$
Latus Rectum ($LR$)$\dfrac{2b^2}{a}$$\dfrac{2a^2}{b}$
Major & Minor Axes$2a$ ($X$-axis), $2b$ ($Y$-axis)$2b$ ($Y$-axis), $2a$ ($X$-axis)
Focal Distance Sum$SP + S'P = 2a$ (Const.)$SP + S'P = 2b$
Dhyan rakho: $SP + S'P = 2a$ is mechanical definition used to draw ellipse using two pins and a loop of string!
#Auxiliary Circle & Parametric Representation
Auxiliary circle: Circle described on major axis as diameter: $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$.
Let $Q(a\cos\theta, a\sin\theta)$ be on auxiliary circle, then corresponding point $P$ on ellipse is:
$P(\theta) \equiv (a\cos\theta, \; b\sin\theta), \quad \theta \in [0, 2\pi)$
Angle $\theta$ is Eccentric Angle (NOT polar angle of $P$, it is the angle made by $OQ$ with $X$-axis!).
Ex.6 : Find eccentricity, foci, and latus rectum of $9x^2 + 16y^2 = 144$.
Dividing by 144: $\dfrac{x^2}{16} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1 \implies a^2 = 16, b^2 = 9 \implies a = 4, b = 3$ ($a > b$).
$e = \sqrt{1 - 9/16} = \dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{4}$ $\implies$ Foci: $(\pm ae, 0) =$ $(\pm \sqrt{7}, 0)$.
Length of $LR = \dfrac{2b^2}{a} = \dfrac{2(9)}{4} =$ $\dfrac{9}{2}$.

#Line and Ellipse: Condition of Tangency
Line $y = mx + c$ is tangent to $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ iff:
$c^2 = a^2 m^2 + b^2 \implies c = \pm \sqrt{a^2 m^2 + b^2}$
Point of contact: $\left(-\dfrac{a^2 m}{c}, \; \dfrac{b^2}{c}\right)$ where $c = \pm \sqrt{a^2 m^2 + b^2}$.
#Forms of Tangents to Ellipse
1. Slope Form: $y = mx \pm \sqrt{a^2 m^2 + b^2}$
2. Parametric Form ($ heta$): $\dfrac{x\cos\theta}{a} + \dfrac{y\sin\theta}{b} = 1$
3. Point Form ($x_1, y_1$): $T = 0 \implies$ $\dfrac{x x_1}{a^2} + \dfrac{y y_1}{b^2} = 1$
#Director Circle of Ellipse
Locus of point of intersection of two mutually perpendicular tangents to the ellipse:
$x^2 + y^2 = a^2 + b^2$
Concentric circle with center $(0,0)$ and radius $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$.
#Important Tangent Properties
Product of Perpendiculars from foci $S, S'$ onto ANY tangent is constant: $p_1 \cdot p_2 = b^2$!
Feet of perpendiculars from foci onto any tangent lie on Auxiliary Circle: $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$.
Ex.7 : Find tangents to $x^2 + 4y^2 = 4$ having slope $m = 1$.
Standard form: $\dfrac{x^2}{4} + \dfrac{y^2}{1} = 1 \implies a^2 = 4, b^2 = 1$. With $m = 1$:
$c = \pm \sqrt{a^2 m^2 + b^2} = \pm \sqrt{4(1)^2 + 1} = \pm \sqrt{5}$.
Tangents: $y = x \pm \sqrt{5}$ $\implies x - y \pm \sqrt{5} = 0$.

#Equations of Normals to Ellipse
1. Point Form ($x_1, y_1$): $\dfrac{a^2 x}{x_1} - \dfrac{b^2 y}{y_1} = a^2 - b^2$
2. Parametric Form ($ heta$): $ax\sec\theta - by\csc\theta = a^2 - b^2$
3. Slope Form ($m$): $y = mx - \dfrac{m(a^2 - b^2)}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 m^2}}$
At most 4 normals can be drawn from an external point to an ellipse.
#Chords of Ellipse
1. Chord joining $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$:
$\dfrac{x}{a}\cos\left(\dfrac{\theta_1 + \theta_2}{2}\right) + \dfrac{y}{b}\sin\left(\dfrac{\theta_1 + \theta_2}{2}\right) = \cos\left(\dfrac{\theta_1 - \theta_2}{2}\right)$
If this chord passes through focus $(ae, 0)$, then: $\tan(\theta_1/2) \cdot \tan(\theta_2/2) = \dfrac{e - 1}{e + 1}$.
2. Chord of Contact ($T = 0$): $\dfrac{x x_1}{a^2} + \dfrac{y y_1}{b^2} = 1$
3. Chord with Midpoint $(x_1, y_1)$: $T = S_1$ $\implies \dfrac{x x_1}{a^2} + \dfrac{y y_1}{b^2} = \dfrac{x_1^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y_1^2}{b^2}$
#Reflection Property of Ellipse
A ray emanating from one focus $S$, after reflection from the inner surface of the ellipse, passes through the other focus $S'$.
Normal at point $P$ is internal bisector of $\angle SPS'$, and tangent is external bisector!
Ex.8 : Find equation of normal to $\dfrac{x^2}{16} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$ at $(2, 3\sqrt{3}/2)$.
Here $\theta = \pi/3$. Using parametric form: $ax\sec\theta - by\csc\theta = a^2 - b^2$:
$4x\sec(\pi/3) - 3y\csc(\pi/3) = 16 - 9 = 7 \implies 4x(2) - 3y(2/\sqrt{3}) = 7$.
$8x - 2\sqrt{3}y - 7 = 0$.

#Hyperbola: Standard Equation & Terminology
Standard equation: $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ ($e > 1$).
FeatureStandard: $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$Conjugate: $-\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$
Eccentricity ($e$)$b^2 = a^2(e^2 - 1) \implies e = \sqrt{1 + b^2/a^2}$$a^2 = b^2(e'^2 - 1) \implies e' = \sqrt{1 + a^2/b^2}$
Foci ($S, S'$)$(\pm ae, 0)$$(0, \pm be')$
Directrices$x = \pm \dfrac{a}{e}$$y = \pm \dfrac{b}{e'}$
Latus Rectum ($LR$)$\dfrac{2b^2}{a}$$\dfrac{2a^2}{b}$
Focal Distance Diff.$|SP - S'P| = 2a$$|SP - S'P| = 2b$
#Relation Between Eccentricities of Conjugate Hyperbolas
If $e$ and $e'$ are eccentricities of a hyperbola and its conjugate hyperbola:
$\dfrac{1}{e^2} + \dfrac{1}{e'^2} = 1$
Super frequent direct JEE formula! If $e = 2$, then $1/e'^2 = 1 - 1/4 = 3/4 \implies e' = 2/\sqrt{3}$.
#Parametric Coordinates of Hyperbola
Point on $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$: $P(\theta) \equiv (a\sec\theta, \; b\tan\theta)$, $\theta \in [0, 2\pi) \setminus \{\pi/2, 3\pi/2\}$.
Ex.9 : If eccentricity of $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ is $e = 5/4$, find eccentricity of conjugate.
Using $\dfrac{1}{e^2} + \dfrac{1}{e'^2} = 1 \implies \dfrac{16}{25} + \dfrac{1}{e'^2} = 1 \implies \dfrac{1}{e'^2} = \dfrac{9}{25}$.
Thus $e'^2 = \dfrac{25}{9} \implies e' =$ $\dfrac{5}{3}$.

#Tangents to Hyperbola ($dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$)
1. Condition of Tangency: Line $y = mx + c$ is tangent iff $c^2 = a^2 m^2 - b^2$
Requires $|m| > b/a$ for real tangents! If $|m| = b/a$, line is parallel to asymptotes.
2. Slope Form: $y = mx \pm \sqrt{a^2 m^2 - b^2}$
3. Parametric Form ($ heta$): $\dfrac{x\sec\theta}{a} - \dfrac{y\tan\theta}{b} = 1$
4. Point Form ($x_1, y_1$): $T = 0 \implies \dfrac{x x_1}{a^2} - \dfrac{y y_1}{b^2} = 1$
#Director Circle of Hyperbola
Locus of point of intersection of two perpendicular tangents:
$x^2 + y^2 = a^2 - b^2$
If $a > b$: Real circle with radius $\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$.
If $a = b$: Point circle $(0, 0)$ (tangents only intersect perpendicularly at origin).
If $a < b$: No real perpendicular tangents exist (imaginary director circle).
#Normals to Hyperbola
1. Point Form ($x_1, y_1$): $\dfrac{a^2 x}{x_1} + \dfrac{b^2 y}{y_1} = a^2 + b^2$
2. Parametric Form ($ heta$): $ax\cos\theta + by\cot\theta = a^2 + b^2$
3. Slope Form ($m$): $y = mx \pm \dfrac{m(a^2 + b^2)}{\sqrt{a^2 - b^2 m^2}}$
Ex.9b : Tangent to $\dfrac{x^2}{9} - \dfrac{y^2}{4} = 1$ with slope $m = 2$:
$c = \pm \sqrt{a^2 m^2 - b^2} = \pm \sqrt{9(4) - 4} = \pm \sqrt{32} = \pm 4\sqrt{2}$. Tangent: $y = 2x \pm 4\sqrt{2}$.

#Asymptotes of Hyperbola
Straight lines passing through center and tangent to hyperbola at infinity:
$y = \pm \dfrac{b}{a}x \iff \dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 0$
Angle between asymptotes: $2\theta = 2\tan^{-1}(b/a) = 2\sec^{-1}(e)$.
Differs by Constant Rule: For any hyperbola $H$, its asymptotes $A$, and conjugate $C$:
$H + C = 2A$ $\implies (H - A) + (C - A) = 0$ (They differ only in constant term!)
Product of perpendiculars from ANY point on hyperbola to asymptotes: $\dfrac{a^2 b^2}{a^2 + b^2}$.
#Rectangular Hyperbola ($a = b \iff e = \sqrt{2}$)
Asymptotes are perpendicular ($x^2 - y^2 = 0 \implies y = \pm x$).
Rotated Form: $xy = c^2$ (Axes rotated by $45^\circ$, $c^2 = a^2/2$)
Parametric point: $P(t) \equiv \left(ct, \; \dfrac{c}{t}\right)$, $t \neq 0$.
Tangent at $t$: $x + yt^2 = 2ct$ $\iff \dfrac{x}{t} + yt = 2c$.
Intercepts on axes: $A(2ct, 0)$ and $B(0, 2c/t)$. Area of $\triangle OAB = \dfrac{1}{2}(2ct)(2c/t) = 2c^2$ (Constant!). Point $P$ bisects $AB$!
Normal at $t$: $xt^3 - yt = ct^4 - c$.
Ex.10 : If a triangle has vertices on $xy = c^2$, where does its orthocenter lie?
Let vertices be $t_1, t_2, t_3$. Orthocenter is $H\left(-\dfrac{c}{t_1 t_2 t_3}, \; -c t_1 t_2 t_3\right)$.
Notice: $x_H \cdot y_H = (-c/T)(-cT) = c^2 \implies$ Orthocenter also lies on the hyperbola!

#Common Tangents Across Different Conics
Systematic recipe to find common tangent between two curves:
1. Write slope-form tangent of Conic 1: e.g. for $y^2 = 4ax \implies y = mx + a/m$.
2. Apply condition of tangency for Conic 2:
$\to$ For circle $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$: perpendicular distance from center $= r \implies \dfrac{|a/m|}{\sqrt{1+m^2}} = r$.
$\to$ For ellipse $x^2/A^2 + y^2/B^2 = 1$: equate $c^2 \implies (a/m)^2 = A^2 m^2 + B^2$.
3. Solve the polynomial for $m$, then write the lines.
#Pole and Polar
For point $P(x_1, y_1)$ (Pole), its Polar with respect to any conic $S = 0$ is $T = 0$.
If $P$ is outside, Polar is the Chord of Contact.
If $P$ is on the curve, Polar is the Tangent.
Polar of Focus is the Directrix!
Conjugate Points: If polar of $P$ passes through $Q$, then polar of $Q$ passes through $P$.

#Kaunsa Method Lagaun ?
Jab question me yeh dikhe...Toh direct yeh method lagao !
Common tangent between 2 conics$\Rightarrow$ Slope form $y = mx + c$ likho aur dusre conic ki tangency condition se $m$ nikaalo.
Chord with given midpoint $(x_1, y_1)$$\Rightarrow$ Seedha $T = S_1$ lagao (Parabola, Ellipse, Hyperbola sabke liye valid!).
Focal chord property / length$\Rightarrow$ Parametric formula $t_1 t_2 = -1$ aur length $= a(t + 1/t)^2 = 4a\csc^2\theta$.
Locus of intersection of $\perp$ tangents$\Rightarrow$ Director circle: Parabola $\to x+a=0$, Ellipse $\to x^2+y^2=a^2+b^2$, Hyp $\to x^2+y^2=a^2-b^2$.
Normals from point $(h, k)$ to parabola$\Rightarrow$ Cubic $am^3 + (2a-h)m + k = 0$ banao, $\sum m_i = 0$ aur $\sum y_i = 0$ use karo.
#Last Minute Formula Strip
Parabola Tangent
$y = mx + \dfrac{a}{m}$
$P = (a/m^2, 2a/m)$
Parabola Normal
$y = mx - 2am - am^3$
Foot $= (am^2, -2am)$
Ellipse Tangent
$y = mx \pm \sqrt{a^2 m^2 + b^2}$
Director: $x^2+y^2=a^2+b^2$
Hyperbola Tangent
$y = mx \pm \sqrt{a^2 m^2 - b^2}$
Director: $x^2+y^2=a^2-b^2$
Conjugate Hyp. $e$
$\dfrac{1}{e^2} + \dfrac{1}{e'^2} = 1$
Direct formula!
Rect. Hyp. $xy=c^2$
$x + yt^2 = 2ct$
Area $\triangle OAB = 2c^2$
#Dhyan Rakho ! (Common Galtiyan)
Vertical Parabola / Ellipse Trap: Parabola $x^2 = 4ay$ me tangent $y = mx - am^2$ hoti hai, $y = mx + a/m$ mat laga dena!
Hyperbola Tangent Slope Condition: Hyperbola me real tangent ke liye $|m| > b/a$ hona zaroori hai. Agar $|m| \le b/a$, koi tangent exist nahi karti!
Focal Distance Definition: Ellipse me sum $SP + S'P = 2a$ hota hai, jabki Hyperbola me difference $|SP - S'P| = 2a$ hota hai.
Director Circle of Hyperbola: Agar $a < b$ ho, toh hyperbola ka koi real director circle nahi hota (radius $\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$ imaginary ho jati hai)!

Q1. Feet are $(am_i^2, -2am_i)$. $\sum m_i = 0 \implies y_G = 0$. $x_G = \frac{a}{3}(\sum m_i^2) = \frac{a}{3}(-2\sum m_1 m_2) = \frac{2h-4a}{3}$.

#Advanced Me Kya Dhyan Rakhein
Parametric form over Cartesian: Variable tangents or chords me parameter $t$ or $\theta$ use karo. Calculations $3\times$ fast ho jaati hain!
Homogenization Shortcut: Chord subtending $90^\circ$ at origin? Homogenize conic with line $lx + my = 1$ and set coeff($x^2$) + coeff($y^2$) $= 0$.
Auxiliary Circle & Projection: Ellipse area/tangent problems ko auxiliary circle par project karke solve karo ($\text{Area}_{\text{ellipse}} = \frac{b}{a} \times \text{Area}_{\text{circle}}$).
